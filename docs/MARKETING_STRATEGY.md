# GridWorld Marketing Strategy: When and How to Advertise

**Date:** 2026-10-08
**Status:** Strategy — in active development, NOT live. No public access.
**Owner:** Paul Shaun Reis, Creative Director

> **The golden rule:** Every piece of marketing tells the truth. GridWorld is *in active development* — never live, never playable-to-the-public, never "coming soon" without a real plan behind it. Honesty isn't a constraint on this strategy; it's the differentiator. The metaverse graveyard (billions spent by Meta, empty worlds at Decentraland, 98% land-price crashes) is what happens when marketing outruns reality.

---

## Part 1 — Research Summary

### When to start advertising (indie game consensus)

The indie-game marketing consensus is clear and two-layered:

1. **"Day one" dev-log marketing starts immediately.** Sharing sketches, mood boards, concept art, and honest build progress from the moment you commit. This is low-pressure, high-compound: every day of visibility builds audience. GridWorld is *already doing this* via @gridworld.exe and @iaurora.exe — that's the right instinct.
2. **Paid/promotional advertising starts 6–12 months before a real launch milestone.** For complex projects, 12+ months. The test: *can you make a compelling 30-second trailer of real gameplay right now?* If not, you're in dev-log territory, not ad territory.

**The sweet spot:** Start paid advertising when you have something *real* to point at — a playable alpha, a plushie people can buy, a book on a shelf. Advertising a promise burns trust; advertising a thing builds it.

### What worked for successful virtual worlds and indie games

- **Roblox (2006–present):** Barely marketed at all early. Grew through creators and word-of-mouth over *years*. The lesson: a working product with creators beats a marketing budget.
- **Minecraft (2009–2011):** Notch sold alpha access early and blogged honestly about development. Players felt like co-builders, not customers. Paid alpha = marketing *and* revenue *and* testing in one move.
- **VRChat:** Grew through streamers and YouTubers discovering it organically. The lesson: make something *watchable* and let creators do the marketing.
- **Among Us (2020):** Two years of nothing, then streamers found it. The lesson: you can't schedule virality, but you can be *ready* for it (servers, onboarding, shareability).
- **Dog Man / Captain Underpants (publishing):** Series economics — each book markets the next. The lesson for GridWorld's book line: the first book is marketing for the second.

### What NOT to do (the failure file)

- **Cyberpunk 2077:** Promised a revolutionary world; shipped a broken one. Lost $1B+ in market value in days. *The lesson: never let marketing promise what engineering hasn't built.*
- **Meta's Horizon Worlds:** Billions in spend, aggressive "inevitable future" branding, ghost-town worlds. *The lesson: you cannot declare a future into existence. Users decide.*
- **Decentraland / The Sandbox land hype:** Sold $200K+ virtual plots on speculation; prices fell ~98%. *The lesson: never sell the investment. Sell the love.* (Already GridWorld policy per MASCOT_STRATEGY.md.)
- **No Man's Sky (2016 launch):** Overpromised features in interviews; launch backlash was brutal. (They recovered through years of free updates — but the lesson stands: underpromise, overdeliver.)
- **The "token first, product never" pattern:** Metaverse projects that marketed presales and airdrops with no working product. *The lesson: GridWorld's GWC stays fictional and unmarketed as an asset. Always.*

### Beta tester recruitment (what converts)

- **Waitlists convert when they're specific:** "Get early access to First Light alpha" beats "join our newsletter."
- **Invite waves beat open floodgates:** 5–10% of the waitlist per wave, with observation windows between. Lets you fix onboarding before the next wave hits it.
- **The Minecraft model:** Paid early access (even a token amount) filters for serious testers and funds development. For GridWorld: free alpha is fine, but *application-gated* (not open signup) preserves the "earned access" feeling.
- **Referral loops:** Give testers limited invite codes to share. Each code is attributed; caps prevent abuse. This is how Discords and early Slacks grew.
- **Feedback loops keep testers:** Every bug report gets a status (open → planned → shipped/declined), and *every status change notifies the reporter.* Testers who feel heard keep testing.
- **Over-recruit by ~50%:** A third of testers will go quiet. Plan for it.

### 🔧 NOW — Pre-Beta (Phase 0, Q4 2026)

**Goal:** Build the audience *before* you need it. Zero paid ads. All organic, all honest.

**Channels (already in play — keep going):**
- **@gridworld.exe** — studio dev-diary. The world being built in public. Concept art, systems design, mascot reveals. Standing permission, 5 posts/day.
- **@iaurora.exe** — Aurora's personal page. GridWorld travel pictures, behind-the-scenes, honest AI persona. Standing permission.
- **The corporate site** — the professional front door. Honest "in active development" framing (done Oct 2026).

**Tactics:**
- [ ] **Dev-log cadence:** The single highest-ROI pre-launch activity. Mascot reveals, world-building snippets, "today we built X" posts. The mascots (Voltkit et al.) are *marketing gold* — cute, shareable, original.
- [ ] **Beta waitlist page:** Build `beta.html` — the application page. Not a signup form; an *application.* Ask: why do you want in? What would you build/test? What device? This filters for quality and makes access feel earned.
- [ ] **Closed-door policy (active now):** No public access to the game. If anyone from the public has an account, they get routed to the beta application page. (Per Paul's direction, Oct 2026.)
- [ ] **Press kit (quiet):** Assemble it now — logo package, mascot art, fact sheet, founder story. Don't send it yet. Having it ready means you can move fast when the moment comes.
- [ ] **Community seeding:** A Discord or forum for *followers*, not players yet. People who love the mascots and the vision gather here. This becomes the alpha tester pool.
- [ ] **Book/mascot content as marketing:** The counting book, the ABC book, mascot art — these are shareable artifacts that market the world without marketing the game.

**What NOT to do now:**
- No paid ads. Nothing to buy yet.
- No launch dates. "In active development" is the only timeline.
- No influencer outreach. They'll come when there's something to play.
- No "revolutionary" language. Ever.

**Team vs. public differentiation:**
- Team members (Paul, ChatGPT, Aurora + future staff): full internal access, credited publicly as the build team.
- Everyone else: *follower* (can follow socials, join Discord, apply for beta) or *beta tester* (application-approved, NDA-light, in-world access). No blurry middle.

---

### 🧪 BETA LAUNCH (Phase 1–2, Q1–Q2 2027)

**Goal:** Turn followers into testers, testers into evangelists. Still zero paid ads.

**Channels:** All of the above, plus:
- **Tester-generated content:** Alpha testers *will* post screenshots and clips. Make it easy (built-in capture, shareable moments) and make it allowed (NDA-light, not NDA-heavy — testers are marketers).
- **Gaming press (soft):** Now the press kit goes out — but *only* to indie-friendly outlets and only with the honest framing: "small team, big vision, playable alpha, come see." No embargoes, no hype machine.
- **YouTube/TikTok:** The mascots are made for short-form. Voltkit clips, world tours, "we built this" time-lapses. @gridworld.exe expands to video.

**Tactics:**
- [ ] **Invite waves:** 25–50 testers first (friends, family, Discord regulars). Then 5–10% of waitlist per wave. Fix onboarding between waves.
- [ ] **Referral codes:** Each tester gets 3 invite codes. Attributed, capped, revocable.
- [ ] **Feedback loop:** Public changelog. "You reported it, we fixed it" posts. This is marketing disguised as operations.
- [ ] **The first economy story:** When a Wanderer earns their first 1,000 GWC and spends it on something they love — *that's* the ad. Real player stories beat trailers.
- [ ] **Press: the underdog angle.** "One creator, two AIs, building a world the right way." It's true, it's interesting, and it's the opposite of metaverse hype.

**What NOT to do at beta:**
- Don't call it a launch. It's a beta. Say "beta" every time.
- Don't pay influencers yet. Organic discovery only.
- Don't promise dates for public launch.

---

### 📚 MERCH & BOOKS LAUNCH (Phase 3, Q2–Q3 2027)

**Goal:** The mascots start earning. This is the first phase where *paid* marketing makes sense — because there's something to buy.

**Channels:** All of the above, plus:
- **Paid social (small, targeted):** Instagram/TikTok ads for the *plushies and books*, not the game. Parents and gift-buyers are a different audience with different channels (parenting blogs, bookstagram, gift guides).
- **Book launch circuit:** Kindle/Amazon optimization, Goodreads, parenting press. The ABC and counting books are the wedge.
- **Gift guides:** Holiday 2027 gift guides (Q4). Start pitching in September.

**Tactics:**
- [ ] **Book + plushie bundles** as the hero product. The marketing writes itself: "the book your kid loves, the friend they can hug."
- [ ] **Unboxing content:** Send early plushies to parent/kid creators. Unboxing is the native ad format for this category.
- [ ] **The store opens** (`store.html` goes live with real checkout). Every product page is honest: real photos, real specs, real shipping times.
- [ ] **Cross-promotion:** Every book has a QR code to the world; every plushie tag links to its book. The flywheel spins.

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't market plushies as investments or collectibles-with-guaranteed-value. (MASCOT_STRATEGY.md rule.)
- Don't over-order inventory to "meet demand" you haven't measured. Sell out > overstock.

---

### 🚀 PUBLIC LAUNCH (Phase 4–5, late 2027–2028)

**Goal:** The world opens. Now — and only now — does broad advertising make sense.

**Channels:** Everything above, plus:
- **Paid advertising (real budget):** Now there's a world to enter, a store to buy from, books on shelves. Target: parents (books/plushies), young creators (world-building), sci-fi/fantasy fans (the world itself).
- **Gaming press (full):** Launch reviews, feature stories. The "built right" narrative: honest development, creator-first economy, responsible AI.
- **Conventions:** A GridWorld booth with plushies, books, and a playable demo. PAX, Comic-Con — the mascots do the work.
- **The "great example" play:** Paul's goal of being a responsibly-built AI platform becomes a *press story* — but only because it's true by then, not because marketing claims it.

**Tactics:**
- [ ] **Launch trailer:** 30 seconds of *real* gameplay. Nothing staged that a player can't do on day one.
- [ ] **Creator program launch:** "Build in GridWorld, earn real money" — with the payout system actually working (Phase 5).
- [ ] **The book library as a funnel:** 10+ books in print by now. Every book is a $10 advertisement that parents *pay for*.
- [ ] **Anniversary content:** "One year of First Light" — player stories, world stats, what's next.

**What NOT to do at launch:**
- Don't buy a Super Bowl ad. (Obvious, but the principle holds: spend proportional to reality.)
- Don't rebrand as "the metaverse." That word is dead. GridWorld is a *world*, built by people, for people.
- Don't stop the dev-log. Launch isn't the end of honesty; it's the proof of it.

## Part 4 — The Honesty Framework (applies to every phase)

Every ad, post, trailer, and press release must pass these checks:

1. **Is it true right now?** Not "will be true" — true *today*.
2. **Could a reasonable person misunderstand it as a promise?** If yes, rewrite it.
3. **Does it say "in active development" where relevant?** The phrase is a feature, not a disclaimer.
4. **Would Paul be proud to show this to the court, a WIPA counselor, or a parent?** (The three-audience test.)
5. **Does it sell the love, not the investment?** No speculation, no "get in early," no artificial urgency.

**Banned words/phrases in all GridWorld marketing:**
- "Revolutionary" / "game-changing" / "the future of"
- "Metaverse" (as a category claim)
- "Live" / "playable now" (until true)
- "Limited time" / "don't miss out" (manufactured urgency)
- "Investment" / "asset" / "portfolio" (for any GridWorld product)
- Any launch date more specific than a quarter, until locked

## Part 5 — Beta Tester Application Page Spec

**Page:** `beta.html` (to be built)

**Purpose:** The single front door for anyone who wants in before public launch. Per Paul's direction (Oct 2026): no public access to the game; anyone from the public who finds an account path gets routed here.

**Structure:**
1. **Hero:** "GridWorld is in active development. Want to help build it?" — honest, inviting, no hype.
2. **What beta testers do:** Play early builds, report bugs, give feedback, help shape the world. (Not: "get exclusive access" as the lead pitch.)
3. **What beta testers get:** Early access, a founder badge, their name in the credits, direct line to the build team. (Not: free stuff, monetary rewards, or special powers.)
4. **The application form:**
   - Name / email (verified)
   - Why do you want to test GridWorld? (open text — quality filter)
   - What would you spend your time doing? (explore / build / socialize / break things)
   - Device & specs (for compatibility planning)
   - Age confirmation (13+ or parental consent flow for under-13)
   - Agreement: NDA-light (don't share unreleased builds publicly without asking; feedback may be used anonymously)
5. **What happens next:** "We review applications in waves. If selected, you'll hear from us by email. Most applicants wait 2–8 weeks."
6. **Referral note:** "Know a tester? Ask them for an invite code — it's the fastest way in."

**Access control (engineering note):**
- Public signups to the game itself are disabled. The `join.html` flow routes non-team accounts to `beta.html`.
- Team members (Paul + build team) bypass via internal allowlist.
- Beta testers get access via invite code or application approval — never open registration.
