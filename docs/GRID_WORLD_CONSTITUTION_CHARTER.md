# Grid World Constitutional Charter

Date: 2026-10-06
Status: Foundational proposal — requires qualified legal review before adoption as a legal instrument

## Preamble
Grid World is designed as an evolving digital society and creative platform. Its rules should protect people, preserve human agency, encourage creation and commerce, and allow the Grid to grow without abandoning its foundations.

This Charter is a governance blueprint, not a substitute for the laws of any country, contract, corporate governance document or legal advice.

## Article I — Reality Supremacy
Real-world law has priority over Grid World rules. Grid World rules govern participation only to the extent permitted by applicable law.

## Article II — Prime Directive
Protect the safety, integrity, lawful operation, human agency and continuity of Grid World while protecting users, staff, custodians and affected people.

## Article III — Human Dignity
Users are people first, accounts second. Grid World should not knowingly use automation to harass, deceive, exploit or manipulate people.

## Article IV — User Rights
Subject to law and reasonable platform controls, users should have clear rules, notice of material enforcement, an appeal path, reasonable privacy, access to their own account information, AI disclosure, translation transparency and protection against unauthorized impersonation.

## Article V — Creator Rights
Creators should retain appropriate rights in original works subject to law, licenses and platform agreements. Grid World should preserve provenance and attribution whenever technically feasible.

## Article VI — Economic Integrity
Currency, land, marketplace inventory, card ownership and rewards must be authoritative, auditable and resistant to duplication or unauthorized manipulation. The system must not promise guaranteed real-world financial returns.

## Article VII — AI
AI is a governed tool/actor category. AI must disclose itself where required, obey Grid World policy, preserve auditability, respect human authority, avoid impersonation and deception, operate only within granted permissions and remain interruptible.

## Article VIII — Custodians
Custodians are stewards, not unlimited authorities. Material decisions should be documented and attributable.

## Article IX — Founder and Staff Protection
Grid World should maintain safeguards including appropriate corporate/entity structure, contracts defining roles and authority, suitable insurance, security controls protecting personal accounts, incident response, legal counsel, documented delegation, records distinguishing personal from corporate decisions, and protection against impersonation and fraudulent instructions.

The platform should not rely on one person as the only security key or administrator.

## Article X — Independent Checks
No single AI, employee or automated process should be able to rewrite the Charter, mint unlimited currency, transfer all land, erase audit history, disable every safety control, remove every administrator or permanently destroy the platform.

## Article XI — Continuity
Grid World should survive staff absence, infrastructure failure, compromised credentials, AI provider failure, regulatory change and major security incidents.

## Article XII — Amendment
Constitutional changes should be versioned, reviewed, announced and preserved. Emergency amendments should expire unless ratified through normal governance.

## Article XIII — International Compatibility
Grid World should seek interoperability with lawful requirements across jurisdictions where it operates. The goal is a strong internal governance system mapped to applicable law jurisdiction by jurisdiction, not a private law that overrides governments.

## Article XIV — Logic Principle
Grid World systems should follow:
RULE -> AUTHORITY -> EVIDENCE -> ACTION -> AUDIT -> APPEAL

If an action cannot explain which rule authorized it, who had authority, what evidence supported it, what happened, how it was logged and how it can be challenged, the action is suspect.

## Founder protection note
Personal founder protection should be implemented through corporate governance, contracts, insurance, access controls, privacy/security measures and qualified legal advice. The Charter should never claim that the founder is immune from law or liability.
